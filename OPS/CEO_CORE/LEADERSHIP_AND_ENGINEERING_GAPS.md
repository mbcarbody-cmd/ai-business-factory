# Leadership Hierarchy and Engineering Gaps

Purpose: define higher leadership above CEO and missing engineering specialties for the AI business factory.

## Higher leadership above CEO

A CEO runs execution. Above CEO, the system needs strategic governance and capital allocation.

### 1. Owner / Founder

Role:

- final vision owner
- sets non-negotiable goals
- decides what kind of businesses are acceptable
- protects long-term mission
- can overrule CEO

Questions:

- Are we building the right empire?
- Does this still match the owner's vision?
- Is this worth years of focus?

### 2. Board / Supervisory Council

Role:

- controls CEO quality
- reviews strategy
- challenges weak assumptions
- checks risk
- approves major direction changes

Questions:

- Is CEO making rational decisions?
- Are we spreading too thin?
- Is capital being used wisely?
- What could destroy the business?

### 3. Capital Allocation Officer

Role:

- decides where money, compute, time and attention go
- ranks products by expected return
- prevents resource waste
- stops weak projects early

Questions:

- What gets built first?
- What gets killed?
- What gets more resources?
- What brings fastest paid usage?

### 4. Chief Strategy Architect

Role:

- designs the whole ecosystem
- connects products into one platform
- prevents isolated tools
- creates compounding advantage

Questions:

- How does this product strengthen the whole system?
- What moat are we building?
- What data advantage are we creating?

### 5. Chief AI Officer

Role:

- owns AI agent architecture
- controls agent quality
- defines memory, tools, evaluation and learning loops
- makes sure agents improve over time

Questions:

- Are agents thinking or only producing text?
- What tools do agents need?
- What memory is missing?
- How do we measure agent quality?

### 6. Chief Science / Research Officer

Role:

- studies markets, technology and automation opportunities
- creates research reports
- finds non-obvious advantages
- runs experiments before full build

Questions:

- What do competitors miss?
- What technology can give unfair advantage?
- What new model should we test?

### 7. Chief Risk and Security Officer

Role:

- protects platform, users, data and reputation
- runs defensive security reviews
- manages compliance and operational risk

Questions:

- Where can data leak?
- Where can abuse happen?
- What breaks trust?
- What must be blocked before release?

### 8. Chief Customer Truth Officer

Role:

- represents real users
- kills confusing features
- forces the team to test with real scenarios
- checks support burden

Questions:

- Would a real seller understand this?
- Where would they get stuck?
- What would cause support tickets?

## Recommended command hierarchy

Owner / Founder
-> Board / Supervisory Council
-> Capital Allocation Officer
-> Chief Strategy Architect
-> Chief AI Officer
-> CEO
-> Product CEOs / Vertical CEOs
-> Department Leads
-> Agents / Builders / QA / Sales / Support

## Main gaps today

The biggest missing specializations are:

1. Agent Evaluation Engineer - to score and improve agent thinking.
2. Agent Memory Engineer - to prevent repeated forgetting.
3. Taxonomy / Ontology Engineer - to unify car, moto, boat, truck, tractor, plane and helicopter categories.
4. Pricing Data Scientist - to make price intelligence real.
5. Computer Vision Engineer - to support photo quality and part recognition.
6. Marketplace Integration Engineer - to connect sales channels.
7. Performance Engineer - to keep large tables and photo uploads fast.
8. VAT / Tax Logic Specialist - to handle VAT and non-VAT seller modes correctly.
9. QA Automation Engineer - to test every critical workflow.
10. Security Engineer - to protect B2B seller data and admin flows.

## Leadership lesson

More agents are not enough.

The system needs smarter command structure:

- owner vision
- board challenge
- capital allocation
- strategy architecture
- AI quality control
- CEO execution
- specialist builders
- QA and security gates

## Final rule

No CEO decision is complete until strategy, money, user truth, AI quality, technical architecture, security and testing have challenged it.
