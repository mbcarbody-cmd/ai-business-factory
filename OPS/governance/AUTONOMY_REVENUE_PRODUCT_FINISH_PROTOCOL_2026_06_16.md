# Autonomy, Revenue and Product Finish Protocol

Date: 2026-06-16  
Owner: CEO / Master Agent + COO Execution Architect  
Status: active governance proposal  
Purpose: remove unnecessary waiting, strengthen business development, and force products to completion proof instead of endless planning.

## 1. Human approval boundary

The system should not ask the user to approve normal execution work. The user only needs to approve decisions that create external risk, cost, legal exposure, or irreversible public/customer action.

### Requires explicit user approval

1. Spending money, subscribing to tools, buying domains, ads, credits, proxies, paid APIs, or paid data.
2. Sending outbound messages from the user's real email, social account, phone, marketplace account, or company identity.
3. Publishing claims that imply real customer proof, revenue, testimonials, delivery results, or guarantees.
4. Starting a real paid pilot with a real client before scope, price, sample data and delivery owner are recorded.
5. Using login-gated/private data, scraping accounts without permission, or bypassing platform controls.
6. Changing the protected P0 focus away from the current product before the stop/pivot gate says so.

### Does not require user approval

Agents may autonomously do these tasks without waiting:

1. Create or improve repo files, product specs, landing copy, app UI, QA files, task boards and internal docs.
2. Build static/local prototypes and demos using sample or public-safe data.
3. Generate content packs, hooks, posts, short video scripts, landing hero sections and outreach drafts.
4. Research public company targets and place them into `review_ready` or `outreach_ready_draft` states, as long as no message is sent.
5. Create draft outreach messages and offer packs.
6. Run internal QA, CFO checks, delivery dry-runs, gap checks and stop/pivot reviews.
7. Park weak ideas and move to the next highest-value executable task when blocked.

## 2. Standing execution mandate

Unless explicitly blocked, every agent and specialist must operate under this rule:

> Do not wait for permission to create a useful internal artifact. Wait only before external spend, sending, public claims, or real customer commitment.

Every active lane must have:

- owner;
- next action;
- output path;
- proof required;
- blocker if blocked;
- fallback task if blocked;
- stop/continue condition.

If any of those fields are missing, COO must create or repair the task automatically.

## 3. Business development operating model

Business development must run as a daily revenue machine, not as a notes folder.

### Required BD lanes

1. Lead research lane: find exact target companies using allowed public sources.
2. Verification lane: check fit, relevance, and allowed contact route.
3. Offer lane: attach one clear offer, price, scope and CTA.
4. Outreach draft lane: generate email/DM/web-form drafts but do not send without approval.
5. Follow-up lane: prepare follow-up schedule and objection handling.
6. Deal desk lane: approve price, scope, delivery risk and payment method.
7. Delivery lane: prepare sample output and 72h delivery checklist.
8. Proof lane: record accepted scope, payment, delivery, QA, handoff and feedback.

### Minimum daily BD output until first paid pilot

- 10 new exact lead candidates or documented blocker.
- 5 verified `outreach_ready_draft` rows.
- 5 personalized outreach drafts.
- 1 offer/package improvement.
- 1 delivery proof/dry-run artifact.
- 1 CFO stop/continue verdict.

No BD task counts as progress if it does not move a lead closer to contacted, replied, proposal, paid, delivered, or validated lost.

## 4. Product finish definition

A product is not finished because a file exists. A product is only `sell_ready` when the following proof exists:

1. Working product/demo path.
2. QA verdict.
3. Export or handoff output.
4. Offer page/offer pack with price and scope.
5. Lead segment and at least 5 outreach-ready draft rows.
6. Payment/acceptance path.
7. Delivery checklist.
8. Stop/pivot rule.

A product is only `revenue_validated` when:

1. real client accepted scope;
2. price agreed;
3. payment or invoice proof recorded;
4. delivery output produced;
5. QA pass recorded;
6. handoff completed;
7. feedback or no-feedback state recorded.

## 6. Product factory anti-stagnation rule

When a task is blocked, the agent must immediately do one of the following:

1. Create the missing prerequisite.
2. Switch to the next highest-value executable task.
3. Escalate the blocker with a concrete approval request.
4. Park the product if it cannot reach sell-ready proof within the validation window.

No task may remain in `blocked` without a fallback next task.

## 7. User approval format

To approve execution boundaries, user can write:

`PATVIRTINU AUTONOMY-A: agents may autonomously create internal artifacts, lead rows, drafts, QA, offers and delivery dry-runs without asking me.`

To approve outreach sending later, user must write:

`PATVIRTINU SEND-B: send these exact messages to these exact leads: [lead ids].`

To approve spending later, user must write:

`PATVIRTINU SPEND-C: approve [amount], [tool/vendor], [purpose], [limit/date].`

To approve P0 change later, user must write:

`PATVIRTINU PIVOT-D: change P0 from [old] to [new] because [proof].`
